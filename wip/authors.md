Our name is flock. We're women and generally use she/her pronouns, though they/them is also occasionally appropriate. 

#### Stuff about us that is useful to keep in mind. 
This is a list of disorders or other conditions we have that are likely to be useful to know about when interacting with us in realspace. This list was compiled 04/03/2026, and is accurate as of that time.  
We have autism spectrum disorder. It's a very high-functioning variant thereof and overall a minor concern.  
We have attention deficit hyperactivity disorder. This means we're largely non-functional without medication. Thankfully, we do have meds for it.  
(Note: In ADHD treatment, there's a general trend of ~85% of patients responding well to at least one of the two major stimulant classes, being methylphenidate and derivatives of amphetamine. We've tried both, and respond significantly better to derivatives of amphetamine.)  
We fidget an incredible amount. This is likely related to the ADHD. Often, we'll end up just randomly dancing while walking around or talking to people.  
We're lactose intolerant.    
(Note: Why do so many meds contain lactose? It's disturbing, this condition is not rare.)   
We have unspecified digestive issues of moderate intensity. These are made worse by ADHD meds.    
We seem to have some sort of gluten intolerance. The exact details of this are unclear, we're currently messing with it.  
We're vaguely depressed. This used to be a lot worse than it is right now, and we're not taking any meds for it at this point, mainly because they didn't actually help. Most of our depression seems to be caused by outside factors, namely our current living situation, and I fully expect it to get better with time.  
We have post orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, or something extremely similar to it. In practice, this means we get dizzy when standing up, can faint if we do it to quickly, and have very low energy a big chunk of the time. Also, our heart rate can skyrocket to an absurd amount when we're going from not moving too much to moving a decent amount in a short period of time. We cannot consume coffee or any other concentrated form of caffeine.    
We often get sufficiently dizzy to lose vision for a few seconds.   
We have severe hyperacusis. This is probably the most significant disability we have, in terms of day-to-day negative impact on our lives. We very quickly get dizzy around even moderately loud or high-pitched noises, and can pass out if it's sufficiently bad. Vomiting can also happen. It's also incredibly painful.   
We have tinnitus. This means we sometimes experience short, somewhat debilitating spikes of pain.   
We have some type of anxiety disorder. If you find us skittishly looking over our shoulder, constantly glancing to see if doors are opening, or jumping at the slighest sound, that's generally normal.  
We have a fine manual dexterity disorder, mainly of the hand. In practice, this means we're very clumsy sometimes.
